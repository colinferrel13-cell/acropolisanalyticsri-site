About Acropolis

About Acropolis Analytics Research Institute.

Acropolis develops independent public-health research and provides rigorous analytic support to academic, public-sector, clinical, and mission-driven partners. Both functions use the same standards for reproducibility, transparent uncertainty, and responsible communication.

Mission & approach

One evidence standard across two connected functions.

Independent research builds reusable programs and public resources. Applied analytics helps partners answer defined questions without separating technical rigor from real-world use.

01

Structure before scale

Build documented, reusable data foundations before adding complex models or making broad claims.

02

Methods in context

Treat survey design, missingness, bias, geography, and uncertainty as part of the result rather than footnotes.

03

Translation by design

Plan maps, reports, and decision tools alongside the analysis so the work remains usable beyond a technical audience.

Colin Ferrel, MPH · Founder
Founder

Colin Ferrel, MPH

Colin Ferrel is an epidemiologist and nanomedicine researcher who founded Acropolis Analytics Research Institute to connect rigorous bench science with decision-focused public-health analytics.

His work spans modular nanotechnology, infectious disease, chronic disease surveillance, cognitive decline, caregiver burden, and population-level risk. Across those domains, the common thread is the development of adaptable research tools that can move between scientific questions and practical settings.

Colin holds bachelor's degrees in Psychology and Medical Microbiology and an MPH in Infectious Diseases/Zoonoses from Kansas State University. His graduate training also includes Agricultural Biosecurity Research, Applied Statistics, and Interdisciplinary Medical Sciences.

He spent five years at the Nanotechnology Innovation Center of Kansas State, developing hybrid nanoparticle platforms that combine biological membranes with synthetic lipid systems to improve biocompatibility, targeting, and drug delivery.
