What we can work on

Start with your question.

These are examples of how an engagement can be scoped. The data, intended audience, and decision determine which methods and deliverables make sense.

01 / Population health & geography

Where is disease burden highest?

Compare places or populations using surveillance, census, or vital statistics data. We look at whether the measures, denominators, and time periods are comparable before mapping differences.

Possible deliverables

  • A county-level map and accompanying rate table
  • An analysis of trends, risk, or geographic differences
  • A data dictionary and notes on uncertainty
02 / Surveys & program evaluation

What did our survey tell us?

Understand what respondents reported, whose experience may be missing, and which comparisons the survey supports. If collection has not started, we can review questions and plan the analysis first.

Possible deliverables

  • A questionnaire review and analysis plan
  • Response summaries with appropriate weighting
  • A repeatable reporting workflow for later survey rounds
03 / Reporting & communication

How do we explain the findings?

A public audience, a program director, and a technical reviewer need different levels of detail. We can shape the same analysis for each audience while keeping the sources, interpretation, and limits consistent.

Possible deliverables

  • A public factsheet or executive brief
  • A technical report, methods appendix, or grant methods section
  • Presentation figures, tables, or stakeholder slides
Working together

From question to handoff

Acropolis works with public-health agencies, researchers, care organizations, and mission-driven programs. A project can be a single analysis, a recurring report, or a research collaboration.

  1. Define the decision

    Identify who will use the result, what they need to decide, and what data are available.

  2. Agree on the scope

    Choose methods, deliverables, data safeguards, milestones, and review points together.

  3. Hand over the work

    Review the findings, their limitations, and the documentation needed to interpret or update them.

Tell us about your project

A question, an intended audience, and a timeline are enough for a first conversation. Please do not send personal health information or confidential files.
